Rocketry

Level 1 Rocket

A Level 1 rocket built in a group setting, where I checked the design, assembly, and calculations for a safe landing and recovery. I modeled the design in OpenRocket. The airframe has laser-cut plywood fins and rings, an eyebolt anchors the recovery gear, and PVC build stands hold it while the epoxy fillets cure.

Goal

A TVC Rocket

Thrust vector control steers a rocket by tilting its motor instead of relying on fins alone. A flight computer reads a motion sensor and drives servos that swing the motor mount to hold the rocket on course. I want to design, build, and fly one.

Already have

  • Built a Level 1 rocket, including recovery and assembly
  • C++ for sensors and motors from the robot car
  • SolidWorks modeling and manual mill work for machined mounts

Still to learn

  • Flight control code that holds a heading
  • Motion sensing for attitude
  • Gimbal mount design and servo drive
  • Ground testing before any flight
Goal

A Level 2 Rocket

Level 2 is the next high-power certification after Level 1. It means a larger rocket and a bigger motor, and I want to design, build, and fly one after my Level 1.

Already have

  • Built a Level 1 rocket, from airframe to fin fillets
  • Modeled a rocket design in OpenRocket
  • Laser-cut fins and centering rings, and epoxy bonding

Still to learn

  • Sizing a larger airframe and motor
  • Stronger construction for higher-power flights
  • Level 2 certification requirements
